2023 - 2025

"[The BathMat] has potential to significantly reduce preventable harm and improve care."

Prof Sir Chris Whitty, Chief Medical Officer for England

The BathMat is an inflatable repositioning device, created to help doctors and nurses safely move proned ICU patients suffering from Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS). Proning is the single most effective intervention to improve mortality outcomes in ARDS, yet it significantly increases the risk of pressure sores. NHS guidelines require repositioning every 2-4 hours to mitigate this.

Effectively and safely repositioning intubated, sedated patients is challenging, often requiring 5-7 staff for 30+ minutes. Over 16,500 patients are cared for in this way in the NHS each year, accounting for over half a million staff hours. ARDS-related pressure injuries alone cost the NHS an estimated £105 million annually.

The BathMat is a novel medical device that simplifies patient repositioning via the use of controllable inflatable vessels which makes repositioning safer and quicker for patients and staff. The BathMat is currently undergoing medical trials in 4 hospital trusts across the UK as of June 2025.

Since September 2024 I have been working with the BathMat team on design and production of the first iteration of the device to undergo medical trials. I have been involved in every stage of the design process: including talking to doctors and nurses to understand their needs, system-level design of pneumatic and electronic control, detailed design of PCBs and pneumatic connectors, verification testing to meet MHRA approval, and managing small-scale production of 20 prototype systems delivered to 4 hospitals.

PRESSURE MAPPING SENSOR

View Full Report (PDF) →

For my individual final year design project (Oct 2024 - May 2025) I was also working with the BathMat team on integrating pressure mapping capabilty into future iterations of the device. Pressure mapping data could be valuable for bedside staff to indicate where potential pressure concentrations might cause bed sores to form. This project involved extensive research into pressure mapping technology, and ultimately prototyping a large flexible pressure sensor array with a custom PCB to read the 64 x 64 sensor array.

The pressure sensor is a passive matrix design formed of long horizontal and vertical electrodes sandwiching a piezoresistive polymer layer. Passive matrix sensors suffer from crosstalk due to parasitic current paths, so a signal isolation circuit was implemented on a custom PCB to filter the data. The sensor driving firmware is run by an Arduino Nano, which can scan the 64x64 matrix at 2 Hz. The Arduino sends data over serial to a visualisation python script running on a PC.

Using a second Arduino I also implemented basic automatic control of the inflatable using the live pressure sensor data. This was set to inflate the section of the mat which had the lowest pressure.

This was a really interesting project from start to finish; involving PCB design, data visualisation and processing, and pneumatics. As part of my work with the BathMat team since graduating I have been further exploring integrating pressure mapping technology into the BathMat. My prototype was a success, but it was very labour intensive, not integrated into the inflatable, and the data visualisation was basic.

We are currently applying for a £200k UKRI grant to continue development of the pressure sensing technology, while the main BathMat project undergoes medical trials. Over the last few months I have been collaborating with specialist ink suppliers on screen-printing conductive silver electrodes to better integrate the sensor with the inflatable.
